## Detailed Notes

### 1. Comprehensive Faculty Walkthrough
* **Project Review:** During this session, the faculty conducted an in-depth walkthrough of our project. We demonstrated the application's functionality while the faculty actively inspected our architectural choices, module integration, and the overall quality of the codebase to ensure it met the expected standards.

### 2. Codebase Review: `README.md` and Test Folder
* **Documentation Assessment:** The faculty specifically reviewed our `README.md` file. We discussed the necessity of having clear setup instructions, architectural overviews, and clean formatting so that anyone reviewing the repository can immediately understand and run the project.
* **Testing Strategy:** The `test` folder was also evaluated. The discussion focused on the thoroughness of our testing suite, verifying how well our unit tests covered critical algorithms (such as tree layout calculations and insertion logic), and reinforcing the importance of test-driven maintainability.

### 3. Incorporating Feedback from Peer Groups
* **Cross-Group Learning:** A major piece of advice emphasized by the faculty was to actively listen to the feedback being given to other groups. By paying attention to the critiques, design flaws, or feature suggestions pointed out in peer projects, we can proactively apply those same lessons and improvements to our own codebase.

---

## Key Takeaways
* **Holistic Reviews:** Faculty walkthroughs validate that the entire project ecosystem—including the user interface, documentation, and test coverage—is held to a high professional standard.
* **The Full Package:** A polished `README.md` and a robust `test` folder are not afterthoughts; they are just as critical to a project's success as the core application logic itself.
* **Learn from Others:** Observing the feedback given to peer teams and preemptively applying those lessons to your own work is a highly effective way to identify blind spots and elevate your software's quality.
