## Detailed Notes

### 1. Faculty Feedback and Project Refinement
* **Faculty Discussion:** We took time to discuss the progress of our visualizer project with the faculty. We presented our collaborative efforts and technical milestones, and the feedback we received helped us refine our integration strategies and polish the final application.

### 2. Collaborative Software Engineering
* **Teamwork Dynamics:** Building the visualizer was a highly collaborative effort within Group 3 (Bhavya, Shubham, Pranel, and myself). It strongly highlighted the critical role of teamwork and structured project management in software development.

### 3. The Value of Early Planning and Modular Design
* **Implementation Planning:** By taking the time to define a clear `implementation_plan.md` early on, we were able to divide the workload efficiently and fairly.
* **Parallel Development:** Our modular package structure (model, builder, layout, view) allowed team members to work simultaneously without stepping on each other's toes. For example, one developer could focus entirely on BST insertion algorithms while another refined the JavaFX UI components, significantly reducing the likelihood of Git merge conflicts.

### 4. Build Tools and Environment Consistency
* **Using Maven:** Utilizing Maven as our primary build tool proved to be a massive asset. It streamlined our dependency management (specifically for JavaFX 21) and ensured that every team member had a perfectly consistent local development environment, preventing frustrating "it works on my machine" issues.

### 5. Cross-Module Integration and Communication
* **Integrating the Application:** Throughout the development cycle, effective communication was paramount, especially when the time came to integrate our separately built modules. Successfully hooking up the UI control panel inputs to the backend tree generation logic required close coordination and understanding of each other's code.

---

## Key Takeaways
* **Communication is Key:** Successful software engineering relies heavily on clear communication, rigorous planning, and teamwork, not just writing functional code.
* **Modularization Prevents Conflicts:** Designing a modular architecture upfront allows teams to develop in parallel with minimal merge conflicts.
* **Standardized Environments:** Tools like Maven are invaluable for keeping a team's development environments synchronized and dependency management pain-free.
* **Faculty Guidance:** Reviewing project architecture and team workflows with faculty provides critical outside perspective that improves the final product.
