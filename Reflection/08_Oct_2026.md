## Detailed Notes

### 1. Continuation of Final Presentations
* **Peer Showcases:** Today's session was dedicated to the continuation of the final project presentations. Since our group (Group 3) presented during the previous session, we were able to focus entirely on watching and evaluating the applications built by the remaining teams in the class.

### 2. Analyzing Peer Software Architectures
* **Diverse Approaches:** Observing the remaining presentations offered a valuable opportunity to analyze different software architectures. We saw a wide variety of approaches to UI design, state management, and algorithmic problem-solving that differed significantly from the methods we used for our Binary Tree Visualizer.
* **Feature Inspiration:** Seeing the creative features, polished user interfaces, and unique problem-solving techniques implemented by other groups provided excellent inspiration and highlighted alternative ways to handle complex technical requirements.

### 3. Extracting Lessons from Faculty Critiques
* **Constructive Feedback:** As the faculty reviewed the presenting groups, they provided detailed, constructive critiques on both the underlying codebases and the live software demonstrations. 
* **Identifying Best Practices:** Paying close attention to this feedback helped identify common development pitfalls—such as tightly coupled components, edge-case oversights, or inadequate error handling—and strongly reinforced industry best practices that we can carry forward into our future software engineering careers.

---

## Key Takeaways
* **Value of Observation:** There is immense educational value in stepping back and critically observing how other engineering teams approach software design, planning, and implementation.
* **Architectural Diversity:** There are many valid ways to build an application; exposing oneself to diverse architectural styles fundamentally broadens one's technical toolkit.
* **Applying Broad Feedback:** Faculty critiques directed at peer teams almost always contain universal lessons about code quality, documentation, and user experience that are highly applicable to our own future development practices.
